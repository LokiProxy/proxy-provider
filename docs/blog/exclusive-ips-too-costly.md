## Where Do the Costs Come From?

An exclusive IP means that a single address serves only one user during the same billing period, with no sharing. This model delivers stability, controllability, and traceability, but it also requires the service provider to reserve address resources and bandwidth capacity independently for each user, making its cost structure inherently higher than that of shared IP pools. 

As a result, the price of exclusive IPs is typically determined by three variables: the number of IPs, bandwidth specifications, and usage period.


## The Real Needs of Small and Mid-Sized Businesses

For teams with daily request volumes ranging from a few thousand to several hundred thousand and low concurrency pressure, what they truly need is often not a massive address pool, but a modest number of IPs that are stable over the long term and predictable.

**When selecting a solution, the metrics worth paying closer attention to are:**

· The availability rate of each individual IP
· Whether the IP is exclusive
· Whether the bandwidth is sufficient
· Whether the IP can be renewed after expiration
· Quick adjustment during network fluctuations

By shifting the evaluation criterion from "how many IPs can I get" to "can these IPs reliably support the entire business cycle," budgets can often be reduced significantly.
