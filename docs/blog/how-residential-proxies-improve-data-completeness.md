## The Importance of Data Completeness

Price comparison pages on cross-border [travel platforms](https://www.lokiproxy.com/?utm_t=1&utm_i=52) need to display combinations of multiple departure points, multiple carriers, and multiple cabin classes at the same time. These prices are generated by each site's localization system, and the same route often shows different fares and taxes on sites in different regions. If samples cover only a few regions, or if scraping in certain regions fails for a long time, the comparison results will systematically deviate from the real market. What determines quality is how much was captured and how much was missed, not simply scraping speed.

## The Three Most Common Types of Data Gaps in the Scraping Pipeline

**Excessive concentration of exits:**

When the same group of exits handles all requests, it is easy to hit the target site's access frequency limit, causing an entire batch of tasks to be interrupted and creating gaps in the time dimension.

**Insufficient regional coverage:**

Without exit nodes in the target country, data from that region's site can only be absent, and the comparison results lack a complete regional dimension.

**Pacing that does not match policies:**

If request frequency and concurrency are inconsistent with the site's robots rules and terms of service, it affects both data acquisition and compliance risk.

## How Residential Proxies Improve Data Completeness

**Split tasks by region**: Build independent scraping queues by country or city so that the data sources and failure reasons for each region can be tracked separately.

**Use rotation and stickiness together**: Use sticky connections for processes that need to maintain state, such as list page pagination and detail pages; use rotation for large-scale price scanning to avoid long-term, high-frequency access from a single exit.

**Control concurrency and pacing**: Set concurrency limits and request intervals according to site policies, and keep backoff retries to reduce the chance of interruption.

**Establish a gap backfill mechanism**: Record the region, time, and result of each task, and automatically re-scrape failed segments so completeness can be quantified and validated.

## FAQ

**Q1: How can I tell whether the fare data I scrape is reliable?**

Cross-compare results for the same route at different times and in different regions, and track request success rates and failure reasons by region. Completeness can be quantified before it can be validated.

**Q2: What compliance issues should I pay attention to when scraping?**

Follow the target site's robots.txt and terms of service, collect only publicly displayed information, control request frequency, do not collect personal data, and comply with relevant laws and regulations in your location and the target region.

**Q3: How should I choose between rotation and stickiness?**

Use stickiness for processes that need to maintain state across pages, and rotation for large-scale, stateless scanning. The two can be used together depending on the task type.
