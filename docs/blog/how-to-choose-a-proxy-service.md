## Criterion 1: Resource Coverage and IP Pool Quality

When choosing a proxy service, resource coverage is the first thing to examine. It is advisable to focus on the number of regions, total IP volume, and update frequency disclosed by the provider.

The availability rate and response latency of a proxy service are the most intuitive experience metrics in daily use. Low availability leads to frequent task interruptions, while high latency affects work efficiency. During evaluation, conduct tests across multiple time periods and observe connection performance during peak and off-peak hours.

## Criterion 3: Compliance and Terms of Service

Compliance is an aspect that cannot be overlooked when choosing a proxy service. Verify whether the provider holds legitimate operating qualifications, whether the terms of use are clear, and whether prohibited uses are explicitly stated. Reputable providers require users to comply with local laws and regulations as well as target platform rules, and clearly define responsibility boundaries in their agreements.

When selecting, read the terms of service carefully and avoid providers with vague terms or unclear usage restrictions. Compliant providers have clear explanations of their business models and use scenarios, which is also the foundation for long-term cooperation.

Also check for hidden fees and whether refund policies are reasonable.

## Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Focusing only on price, not quality**

Low-price services may compromise significantly on stability and resource quality, resulting in higher overall costs.

**Pitfall 2: Trusting "unlimited resources" claims**

Resource scale should be supported by specific data; vague marketing claims require caution.

**Pitfall 3: Ignoring compliance terms**

Providers with unclear terms of service may bring legal and business risks, be sure to verify in advance.

**Pitfall 4: Making long-term purchases without trying first**

Try before you buy to avoid waste caused by compatibility issues.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1: Is a larger IP pool always better?**

Not necessarily. IP pool size should be judged in the context of business scenarios; whether coverage regions and IP types match your needs is more important.

**Q2: How can I determine whether a proxy service is compliant?**

Verify provider qualifications, read the terms of service, confirm that prohibited uses are explicitly stated, and comply with local laws and regulations.

**Q3: What should I focus on testing during a trial period?**

Focus on connection stability in target regions, response latency, and peak-hour performance, and verify whether resource coverage matches marketing claims.

**Q4: How do I choose between pay-per-traffic and pay-per-IP billing?**

Businesses with fluctuating usage are suited to pay-per-traffic billing, while businesses with fixed IP requirements can consider pay-per-IP billing. Estimate total costs based on usage.
