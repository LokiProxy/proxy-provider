# IPv4 vs. IPv6 Guide for Cross-Border Businesses

As internet infrastructure continues to evolve, many cross-border businesses find themselves facing a difficult choice when it comes to network access solutions. IPv4 resources are increasingly scarce, while IPv6 is rapidly gaining adoption thanks to its abundant address space. Companies need to choose the IP solution that best fits their own business scenarios.

![1](https://i.postimg.cc/KYH8ZLvD/1.jpg)


## Core Differences Between IPv4 and IPv6

**IPv4** has been around for a long time and offers strong compatibility across the entire network, but its address resources have been exhausted, and the cost of available dedicated IPs continues to rise. This makes it prone to resource bottlenecks when businesses scale up significantly.

**[IPv6](https://www.lokiproxy.com/?utm_t=1&utm_i=52)** offers a massive address pool with lower address acquisition costs, making it well suited to businesses that need a large number of dedicated IPs. At present, the vast majority of mainstream platforms and services already support IPv6, making it the preferred choice for long-term cross-border business expansion.


## When to Prioritize IPv4

If your business connects to legacy systems or niche sites that only support the IPv4 protocol, or if your traffic volume is modest and you only need a small number of IPs, IPv4 remains a reliable option.

Its drawback is that, when deployed at scale, IP resource supply is constrained and procurement costs are relatively high, making it difficult to support continuously growing business volumes.


## Which Cross-Border Scenarios Suit IPv6

When a company needs to carry out data scraping, online business testing, or parallel operation of multiple projects at a large scale with a high volume of dedicated IPs, IPv6 becomes more cost-effective. Its abundant address resources can support horizontal business expansion without being limited by total address availability.

## Selection Recommendations

· Assess the IPv6 support status of your target platforms and interfaces

· Estimate the number of IPs and concurrency levels required by each business module

· Confirm whether you need to keep the same batch of IPs fixed over the long term

· Start with small-scale validation before gradually increasing the share of IPv6
