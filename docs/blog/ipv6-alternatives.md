## The Current State and Applicable Boundaries of IPv4 Proxies

Global IPv4 public addresses entered the stock-limited era long ago. New available resources are scarce, and the cost of recycling and transferring them continues to climb, directly driving up the procurement unit price of IPv4 proxies. For many business teams, the budget pressure of purchasing independent IPv4 addresses in bulk is increasing year by year.

But this does not mean IPv4 proxies have lost their value. A large number of legacy websites, outdated business systems, and certain industry platforms have not yet completed IPv6 adaptation and only support IPv4 access. If your business targets these platforms, IPv4 proxies remain an irreplaceable choice that ensures stable connectivity of your business links.

**Simply put**: IPv4 is suitable for projects where target sites only support IPv4, business concurrency is moderate, and long-term fixed egress is required. If there is no hard requirement for IPv4, more economical options are available.

## How to Make the Right Procurement Decision for Your Business

The core criterion for choosing is not which one is newer, but the protocol compatibility of target platforms and the scale of your business.

### Step 1: First confirm whether your business target sites support IPv6

If the target platform has no IPv6 access capability at all, prioritize keeping IPv4; if the platform supports dual protocols, prioritize evaluating IPv6 to reduce long-term expenses.

### Step 2: Assess the concurrency level

For small-scale operations, the difference between the two options is limited; when you need to continuously maintain thousands of independent egress points and send high-frequency requests, the cost advantage of IPv6 becomes significantly amplified.

### Step 3: Consider a hybrid strategy

Keep a small amount of IPv4 for legacy platforms and migrate main business operations to IPv6, balancing compatibility and cost.
